Quantum Money Will Be Priced in Energy

Every money system answers two questions. Who stops copying? And what is one unit worth? Banks answer both with trust. Blockchains answer the first with consensus and leave the second to the market. Quantum money answers the first with physics. This week I want to talk about the second.

Copying is solved by physics

The no-cloning theorem says an unknown quantum state cannot be copied. That is not a policy or a protocol. It is how the universe works. A quantum banknote carries a state that anyone can verify but nobody can duplicate. No ledger has to be replicated across thousands of machines to stop a double spend, and no central clerk has to approve every transfer. The note itself refuses to be forged.

That changes the economics of security. Today we spend enormous amounts of electricity and labour defending money against copies: reconciliation, fraud teams, mining, audits. When the copy problem moves into physics, that cost collapses. What is left is a cleaner question: what should a unit of money actually measure?

The case for an energy reference

Every real thing in the economy is made with energy. Steel, food, transport, and now intelligence. A modern AI model is, in very concrete terms, a large amount of electricity turned into useful decisions. As Super ASI takes on more of the work in factories, logistics and research, the dominant input to the economy becomes compute, and the dominant input to compute is energy.

So the natural yardstick for future money is not a central bank's decision or a scarce token. It is the energy it takes to do useful work. Picture a quantum note whose value is referenced to a basket of verified energy, measured in kilowatt-hours delivered to the grid or to a datacenter. Prices of goods become prices of the energy and compute that make them. A contract can say exactly what it means: this much value, anchored to this much physical work.

An energy reference is not a promise of any return. It is a measuring stick. It makes money easier to understand, easier to audit and harder to inflate quietly, because nobody can print kilowatt-hours.

Why datacenters become the mint

Quantum money needs infrastructure: quantum memory to hold states, verification nodes to check them, and secure links to move them. Those live in datacenters. The same datacenters run the AI agents that will be the heaviest users of money, because agents will pay each other for data, compute and services millions of times a second.

At Panda ASI we design datacenters that give energy back: waste heat for cities, flexible load that stabilises the grid, and on-site generation that turns surplus into value instead of loss. A datacenter that produces and measures energy is the most natural issuer and verifier of energy-referenced money. The mint of the next century will not look like a vault. It will look like a cooled hall of servers next to a power plant.

Machine payments need unforgeable money

AI agents are already getting wallets. When software pays software, there is no human to notice a strange transaction or call the bank. The money itself must be safe to hold and safe to hand over. Quantum notes fit that job: an agent can verify a note locally, accept it, and pass it on, with physics guaranteeing it was not duplicated along the way. Add an energy reference and every agent in the world shares the same unit of account, whether it runs in Helsinki, Singapore or orbit.

Money that works off-planet

Orbit is not a figure of speech. Our space program looks at what money means when the nearest bank is light-minutes away. A ledger that needs global consensus struggles with that delay. A quantum note that verifies itself does not. And in space, energy is the one resource everyone counts. Solar power collected, stored and spent is the most honest unit of value a colony could have. Energy-referenced quantum money is a currency that can leave Earth.

What has to happen next

This future is not here yet. Quantum memory has to hold states for longer. Verification has to work over ordinary networks. Standards for measuring and attesting energy have to mature, and regulators have to see that physics-based security can be safer than the systems they supervise today. Each of those is an engineering problem, and engineering problems get solved.

The direction is clear. Banks print money. Blockchains copy it to thousands of machines to keep it honest. The next step is money that cannot be copied at all, measured in the energy that powers everything we build. Super ASI will need it, datacenters will issue it, and physics will protect it.
